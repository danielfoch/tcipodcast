Segment 1 · What “break even” leaves out

A normal vacant month and a light repair bill open a gap.

−$358

a month once both are counted. The episode rounds that to about $350.

The setup is rent of $2,500 and other costs of $2,500, so the spreadsheet says zero. $2,500 divided by 12 is $208. Add $150. About $4,200 a year at the episode’s $350 rounding. Principal paydown is real. It does not pay the plumber.

Source: episode round example, Segment 1. These are not a market print. Vacancy average is $2,500 ÷ 12 = $208.33. $208 + $150 = $358. The episode’s “about $350” × 12 = $4,200.

Illustration only. The furnace, turnover, insurance, and the owner’s time are named in the episode and are not given dollar amounts here.

Segment 6 · Stress test the renewal

One point on a $500,000 mortgage is $278 a month.

$3,335

a year, the gap between 4% and 5% on this one mortgage

The episode rounds the payments to about $2,630 and about $2,910, and the gap to roughly $280 a month, over $3,300 a year. Break-even at 4% means that gap comes from somewhere else.

Source: calculated, not a lender quote. $500,000, 25-year amortization, Canadian semi-annual compounding. Monthly rate = (1 + annual/2)1/6 − 1. Result: $2,630.10 at 4% and $2,908.02 at 5%. Difference $277.92 a month, $3,335 a year.

The episode also says most big banks charge the greater of three months’ interest or the interest-rate differential to break a fixed term early. That rule is stated as the episode gives it. Get the number from the lender.

Segment 7 · Same seven, bigger tools

One door and 25 units use the same list.

One property

A spreadsheet, one separate account, a written checklist, a broker, an accountant, a lawyer, and an hour a quarter with yourself.

Five properties

Property-management software. The trades list matters more. Worth having the corporation conversation with the accountant and the lawyer.

25 units

A bookkeeper for the monthly reports, a manager or staff, a real corporate structure, and the lender in the financing conversation.

Source: episode, Segment 7. The seven: know the numbers, separate the money, give it a job, write the systems down, build the team, plan for the bad month, and review quarterly. Selling is a line on that agenda. Commission, a penalty, capital gains, and the land transfer tax already paid are real costs. Run the sell case with the accountant before it is urgent.
